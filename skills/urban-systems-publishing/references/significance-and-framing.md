# Stage 3b - Significance and Framing

Align the **title**, **abstract**, and **contribution/cover-letter pitch** around the same
claim. Different lengths and audiences may change emphasis, but never the evidence,
population, scale, uncertainty, or causal status. An editor may initially see only a short
summary; make the contribution legible there without treating editorial triage as a
predictable formula. Read only the sections needed for the requested deliverable.

## Choose the significance the evidence earns

The original skill's three contribution types remain useful; a paper can combine them.
A careful descriptive or measurement contribution also deserves a clear account of its
value and need not be disguised as a causal or theoretical breakthrough.

| Contribution | What the reader should understand | Evidence needed in the pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Theoretical/conceptual | What changes in an identifiable debate, mechanism, concept, or typology? | The empirical or logical basis and a fair comparison with the actual literature. |
| Policy/population | Who is affected, what matters in practical terms, and what can be learned? | Population, place, period, magnitude, uncertainty, and what the design can say about action. |
| Methodological | What problem becomes tractable or more reliably measured, estimated, or predicted? | Relevant baselines, assumptions, validation, and a path to reuse. |
| Descriptive/measurement | What previously uncertain pattern, distribution, dataset, or measure is established? | Coverage, definitions, measurement validity, uncertainty, and usefulness for the stated audience. |

A bridge has a lead contribution and supporting contributions. Do not relabel the same
regression a methodological advance unless the method is actually new or improved; applying
an established method can instead produce a substantive finding. Do not manufacture a
conceptual frame to fit a venue. Broader significance can come from a well-supported
boundary condition, replication, new measurement, or synthesis, not only a "paradigm shift."

Use [journal-selection.md](journal-selection.md) to test audience fit. A journal's actual
scope and relevant articles matter more than a quantitative/interpretive stereotype.

## Keep claims commensurate with evidence

- **Causal:** specify the effect, population, exposure/treatment contrast, and identification
  assumptions. A spatial model, covariate adjustment, or a small p-value does not by itself
  identify an intervention effect.
- **Associational/descriptive:** report the pattern at the observed level and avoid treating
  it as individual behavior or an intervention effect. Descriptive evidence can inform
  surveillance, priorities, planning, or hypotheses while leaving intervention efficacy
  unresolved.
- **Predictive:** say what was predicted, for which held-out places/times, how well, and
  compared with what baseline. Do not call in-sample fit forecast performance.
- **Conceptual/interpretive:** name the conceptual move, how the material supports it, and
  relevant alternatives or scope limits. Do not imply universal generality from a situated
  account.
- **Methodological:** compare with the appropriate field literature and baselines, state
  conditions where the method works, and disclose failure cases. No corpus keyword search
  establishes that a method is the first of its kind.

See [analysis-rigor.md](analysis-rigor.md) and [spatial-methods.md](spatial-methods.md).

## Build the hinge sentence

For results-led writing, a useful hinge states the finding and the basis on which the
reader can believe it. For example, with **synthetic illustrative numbers only**:

> Across the studied tracts, a 10-percentage-point difference in canopy cover was associated
> with a 0.7-point difference in recorded depression prevalence; the estimate remained
> imprecise and the design did not identify an individual treatment effect.

Use real, supplied estimates and intervals in an actual draft. Replace missing results
with marked placeholders or explain what can be written now. Do not stall all drafting
because a number or concept is unresolved: an outline or partial draft can help clarify
it. Do not fill a placeholder with a plausible-looking result.

For a conceptual paper, a hinge might instead specify how the cases change an account of
urban governance. Describe the analytic work performed by the concept; naming a theorist
alone does not supply it.

## Abstract architecture: check the exact article type

Common shapes are useful starting points, but the current official guide controls headings,
length, citations, and required extra statements. [journal-profiles.md](journal-profiles.md)
explains how to record those requirements. An optional local abstract-length median is a
description of a potentially biased sample, not a rule.

- **Structured research abstracts:** put the question/context, methods, results, and
  interpretation in the venue's required headings. Health journals vary in labels and may
  require funding, public-health implications, or other fields. Match the applicable
  reporting guideline without confusing it with the journal's formatting policy.
- **Unstructured research abstracts:** move from problem/debate to approach, key finding,
  and meaning. Put enough design and uncertainty in the summary to support the claim.
- **Broad-audience summaries:** provide minimal context, identify the gap, state the result,
  and explain its wider implication in language an adjacent-field reader can understand.
  "Here we show" is an optional rhetorical device, not a universal Nature requirement.
  Do not transfer Nature's own summary-paragraph or citation rules to every Nature title.
- **Additional significance, plain-language, or research-in-context statements:** verify
  which element the exact article type requires. PNAS, health journals, and other venues
  use different forms. Keep the central claim consistent while adapting vocabulary.

For qualitative, theoretical, or mixed-methods work, a rigid numeric Results slot may be
inappropriate. Describe the finding in its own evidential terms while satisfying the
actual format. Do not force IMRaD if a different structure fits the work and venue.

## Make the contribution visible without inflation

Useful moves include:

1. Explain how a situated result informs a wider process and where that inference stops.
2. Show how competing explanations differ and which observations discriminate among them.
3. Express magnitude in units the reader understands, with uncertainty and baseline.
4. Identify a transferable concept, measure, dataset, or method and its conditions of use.
5. State a relevant policy or disciplinary connection, rather than merely listing sectors.
6. Explain a live question or replication need without exaggerated urgency or priority.

Counterintuitive results require evidence that the previous expectation was actually held.
Mechanism language requires appropriate support: consistency with a proposed mechanism is
weaker than identifying it. Policy relevance does not require pretending observational
results are experimentally established intervention effects.

## Titles and register

Offer a small set of accurate titles with different useful emphases: finding, problem,
method, or concept. Prefer clarity over stylistic imitation. State the geography or
population when omitting it would overgeneralize. Avoid causal verbs unsupported by the
design and priority claims unsupported by a documented literature check.

Corpus patterns such as shorter titles or more frequent colons may help inspect a venue's
register, but they are neither quality signals nor acceptance levers. Do not prescribe
shortening or removing a colon simply to "move up a tier."

Write in the user's requested language. Translate scientific terms consistently, retain
standard names where useful, and preserve uncertainty and causal qualification across
languages. A translation should not strengthen the claim.

## Deliverable

For a complete framing request, provide:

```text
Audience and article type: [with current format source/date or unresolved items]
Claim and significance: [what is supported, for whom, under what limits]
Title options: [accurate alternatives with concise rationale]
Abstract: [usable draft; factual gaps visibly marked]
Contribution hinge: [one standalone sentence]
Evidence alignment: [any overclaims corrected, missing facts, or unresolved tradeoffs]
```

The user may ask for only one of these. Deliver that part without requiring the full
package or repeating every gate.
