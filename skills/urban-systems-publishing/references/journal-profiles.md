# Journal profiles - 45 starting points

This catalog covers urban/regional, spatial, environmental-health and complex-systems
research. It supplies **audience and contribution hypotheses**,
not current submission specifications, quality rankings, or acceptance probabilities.
Read only the relevant cluster. Confirm a candidate's current scope and article type from
its official site and recent publications before recommending it.

## How to use a profile

The audience grouping is deliberately approximate.
The same journal can be broad for one community and specialized for another. A journal's
article types, editorial areas, and accepted methods may span several epistemic cultures.
Do not infer that a quantitative result lacks theory, that a geography journal rejects
quantitative work, or that all economics papers must identify an empirical causal effect.
Formal theory, measurement, description, prediction, and causal estimation have different
standards of evidence. Ask which contribution this manuscript actually makes.

**Current requirements belong in a dated verification note.** Before using a word count,
abstract structure, display-item/reference cap, data policy, disclosure requirement,
reviewer rule, enquiry policy, indexing status, or fee, consult the exact journal's
current official author guide and article type. Record URL, date, rule, and inclusions
(e.g., whether references count toward the word limit). Distinguish requirements from
recommendations and first-submission rules from accepted-manuscript formatting. If the
page is unavailable or sources conflict, mark the item unresolved; do not replace it with
an old number, a publisher-wide template, or a guess.

## Broad interdisciplinary audiences

| Tag | Journal | Audience and contribution to investigate | Fit question |
|---|---|---|---|
| PNAS | Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences | A contribution intelligible and consequential beyond its immediate specialty. | What can a scientist outside the subfield learn or use, and what evidence supports the breadth? |
| SciAdv | Science Advances | Cross-disciplinary scientific advances, including urban, environmental, behavioral, and population questions. | Does the result offer more than technical improvement within one case or workflow? |
| GEC | Global Environmental Change | Human and policy dimensions of environmental change, including governance, adaptation, vulnerability, and sustainability. | Does the study explain human-environment relations or their wider implications, rather than merely mapping exposure? |
| NatSust | Nature Sustainability | Sustainability across natural/social sciences, engineering, and policy. | Which sustainability problem changes in light of the finding, and how does that travel across disciplinary boundaries? |
| NatHumBeh | Nature Human Behaviour | Human behavior across disciplinary approaches. | Is the behavioral contribution central and supported, rather than attached to a spatial pattern after analysis? |
| LancetPlan | The Lancet Planetary Health | Relationships between human health and environmental or planetary systems. | What does the design establish about health and environmental change, and for whom? |
| LancetPH | The Lancet Public Health | Population-health questions with public-health and policy significance. | Are the population, design, absolute magnitude, uncertainty, and implications clear without overclaiming intervention effects? |
| NatCities | Nature Cities | Interdisciplinary research on cities and urbanization. | Why should urban researchers from other methods or disciplines care, and can they understand the contribution? |

Do not assume these journals share abstract formats, enquiry policies, or publication
models. In particular, primary research in Nature Cities and Nature Sustainability has
subscription and Gold OA routes on the official pages checked below; they are not simply
OA-only because their names begin with Nature.

## Disciplinary debates and substantive fields

| Tag | Journal | Audience and contribution to investigate | Fit question |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSM | Social Science & Medicine | Social-science accounts of health, illness, and health systems; varied quantitative, qualitative, and theoretical traditions. | How does the evidence advance understanding of a social process affecting health? |
| AJPH | American Journal of Public Health | Public-health research, practice, and policy. | What is the population-health relevance, and is the practical implication proportionate to the design? |
| UrbanStud | Urban Studies | Interdisciplinary understanding of urban processes and conditions, with empirical and conceptual contributions. | Does the case speak to a wider urban question rather than only profiling a place? Quantitative work can do this. |
| Addiction | Addiction | Addiction science across substances and behaviors, including policy, epidemiology, treatment, and mechanisms. | Is the substantive addiction contribution clear, with suitable design and transparent funding disclosures? |
| EHP | Environmental Health Perspectives | Environmental determinants of human health and related exposure, toxicological, and epidemiological evidence. | Are exposure, outcome, causal interpretation, uncertainty, and relevance to environmental health properly aligned? |
| RegStud | Regional Studies | Regional development, economic geography, regional economics, governance, and policy; plural methods. | What regional process or debate advances, and why is the regional scale meaningful? |
| TobControl | Tobacco Control | Tobacco/nicotine control science and policy. | Does the work contribute to tobacco control, and has the current funding/conflict policy been checked for this study? |
| EPA | Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space | Spatial economies and economic-geographic debates, including critical and heterodox theory. | What does the evidence change in understanding economy and space? |
| HealthPlace | Health & Place | Place, space, and health, including spatial epidemiology and qualitative health geography. | Is place part of the explanation or interpretation, rather than merely a location label? |
| EconGeog | Economic Geography | Economic-geographic processes and theoretically engaged empirical scholarship. | Does the study develop or challenge an economic-geographic account through substantive evidence? |
| AnnalsAAG | Annals of the American Association of Geographers | Geography across methods, human geography, nature-society, and physical/environmental areas. | Which editorial area and geographic contribution fit? Do not rule out a methods paper for lacking an interpretive argument. |
| RSUE | Regional Science and Urban Economics | Urban and regional economic theory and empirics. | Is the economic question and contribution clear? Are causal claims identified when made? |
| JRegSci | Journal of Regional Science | Regional science, location theory, regional/urban economics, and spatial methods. | Does the study add to a regional-science question or method beyond using a familiar estimator? |
| JUrbEcon | Journal of Urban Economics | Economic accounts of urban processes such as housing, agglomeration, transport, sorting, and local public finance. | Does the theoretical or empirical contribution address an economic mechanism, with appropriate assumptions and reproducibility? |

Health & Place illustrates why a single prestige tier is unhelpful: it is a central venue
in health geography and a specialized audience relative to general population-health or
multidisciplinary journals. Use its readership and the manuscript's contribution, not
inconsistent tier labels, to decide fit.

An earlier local corpus excluded IJERPH by default using a historical indexing/quality
flag. That can alter retrieval coverage and should be disclosed when querying such a
snapshot. It is not a scientific assessment of every article and does not justify a
categorical journal ban. Verify any current indexing or policy claim from the index or
journal's official source and distinguish it from the author's institutional requirements.

## Corpus norms and coverage

Use the optional `scripts/query_corpus.py --norms` with a local corpus whose provenance you
can inspect. Report build date, years, journals, field filters, retrieval limits, excluded
records, deduplication, and abstract coverage. Distinguish a completed retrieval from a
capped sample. Low-reference or missing-abstract records are not necessarily invalid papers.

Descriptive title length, colon use, abstract length, authorship, and reference counts
can suggest conventions. They do **not** establish editorial requirements, causal effects
of framing on acceptance, or the journal's preferred team size. Do not change authorship
to match a median. High citation counts or low publication volume do not prove quality.

For an abstract median, show both the number with abstracts and total records, ideally by
year and article type. High coverage alone does not remove selection or metadata errors.
Do not infer near-full coverage from a publisher's name. Missing or zero reference counts can
be missing metadata rather than real zero-reference articles. Label uncertainty and avoid
using noisy medians as targets that override a current author guide.

## Official sources

Use official journal pages to check scope, article types and submission requirements for
your manuscript. The links below are starting points; consult current pages before making
a submission decision.

- [Urban Studies aims and scope](https://www.urbanstudiesjournal.com/about/): welcomes
  empirical and theoretical social-science contributions and asks for wider urban
  understanding. This supports testing the conceptual contribution without excluding
  quantitative work by default.
- [Annals AAG aims and scope](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/raag21/about-this-journal):
  includes Geographic Methods alongside substantive geographic areas. Check the relevant
  editorial area rather than assuming that the journal accepts only interpretive work.
- [Nature Cities publishing options](https://www.nature.com/natcities/submission-guidelines/publishing-options)
  and [Nature Sustainability publishing options](https://www.nature.com/natsustain/submission-guidelines/publishing-options):
  offer subscription and Gold OA routes for primary research; verify current prices and
  applicable funding arrangements when advising a real submission.
- [Nature Sustainability submission guidelines](https://www.nature.com/natsustain/submission-guidelines)
  link separate scope, content-type, formatting, editorial-policy, publishing-option, and
  enquiry pages. Follow the relevant page for the exact question; a generic family label
  is not enough.
