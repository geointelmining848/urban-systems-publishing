# Manuscript-writing workflows

[简体中文](manuscript-workflows.zh-CN.md) · [Installation and use](../README.md)

Use these prompts with `$urban-systems-publishing` in Codex or
`/urban-systems-publishing` in Claude Code; for other clients, use the invocation in the
[installation guide](installation.md). Replace bracketed fields with your own
research. You can work on one section at a time; a complete manuscript is not required.

## Start from your research

Give the assistant a short research brief:

```text
Research question: [What do you want to understand?]
Study setting and period: [Place, spatial units, population and dates]
Evidence and design: [Data/materials, sampling and methods]
Main finding: [Result with uncertainty, or a qualitative interpretation and its basis]
Contribution: [What does this add to the closest literature?]
Writing task: [Outline, introduction, paragraph, abstract, caption or response]
Readers or journal: [If known]
Discussion language: [Your preferred language]
Manuscript language: [Your preferred language]
```

Share only material you are permitted to share. When a result is not yet available, say
so and ask for placeholders rather than finished empirical claims.

## Plan a paper

> Help me build an outline from this research brief. Propose a central argument and the
> job of each section. Identify the evidence each section needs. Explain which parts of
> my proposed contribution are supported and which still need work.

Use the outline to decide what belongs in the paper. A descriptive, predictive,
interpretive or methodological contribution can be valuable without claiming causality.

## Develop the introduction

> Here are my question, main finding and notes on five papers I have read. Draft a
> four-paragraph introduction: the problem, the relevant debate, the unresolved question,
> and this study's contribution. Use only the references I provide, flag unsupported
> statements, and explain the argument connecting the paragraphs.

Check that cited papers support their assigned claims. A study of a new city or region
needs an explanation of what readers learn from that setting, not just a claim of novelty.

## Explain methods and align claims

> Review this methods paragraph and conclusion together. Check whether the design supports
> the kind of claim I make. My units are [units], exposure is [definition], outcome is
> [definition], and design is [design]. Revise the wording and explain any important
> uncertainty, spatial dependence, scale or individual-versus-area inference issue.

For interview or archival research, provide case selection, evidence, analytic approach
and reflexivity instead of forcing the work into a statistical template. Request an
explanation of assumptions rather than treating a named method as proof.

## Write results, an abstract or a caption

> Use this verified results table to draft the results paragraph and a concise abstract.
> Keep estimates, units, denominators and uncertainty consistent. Separate findings from
> interpretation, and mark any missing information with square brackets.

> Write a stand-alone caption for this map. Explain the variable, units, spatial support,
> time period, data source, legend, missing values and uncertainty where relevant.

Review every number against your own analysis. Do not let a fluent paragraph turn a
cross-sectional association into a causal effect or a scenario into an observed outcome.

## Work across languages

> Discuss your suggestions in Simplified Chinese, but draft the manuscript text in English.
> Preserve my technical meaning, cautious claim strength and citation locations. Explain
> important wording choices in Chinese; do not add evidence or make the argument stronger
> than the supplied results allow.

You can request other language pairs in the same way. Supply preferred terminology for
place names, policy concepts and methods; check terms that lack a direct translation.

## Prepare for submission or respond to a reviewer

> Help assess the fit between my contribution and [journal]. Use its current official
> scope and article requirements when accessible, and explain anything you cannot verify.
> Then help adapt my title and abstract to those readers without changing the evidence.

> Draft a respectful reply to this reviewer comment using only the changes I list.
> Distinguish completed work from work still planned. If I disagree, help explain the
> methodological reason and propose a clear manuscript clarification.

Make the final decisions with your coauthors. Check references, declarations,
authorship and required AI-use disclosures, and follow institutional and journal policies.
